Example

A website for an interior design store. Part by part.

Norrsund Hem is a made-up store that we built to show how we work. Here we go through every part of the website: what we did, why, and what it does for the store. The example site itself is in Swedish.

More online sales and more store visits

The starting point. Three things the website must do.

A local interior design store competes with large online shops. So we built the website around what the store does better than them, and made it easy to buy straight away.

  1. Sell online

    Visitors should find the right product quickly and be able to buy without hesitating.

  2. Bring more people into the store

    Opening hours, address and directions should be easy to find, both on the site and on Google.

  3. Stand out

    Personal interior design advice is something the online giants do not offer. It should be visible and bookable right away.

The walkthrough. Why everything looks the way it does.

  1. 1A clear offer and two ways forward

    What we did
    A short headline about the feeling the store sells, an introduction to what it stocks and two buttons: Shop now and Book design advice. The photo shows products in a real room.
    Why
    Visitors decide within seconds whether they are in the right place. Two buttons catch both the person who wants to buy now and the person who needs help first.
    What it delivers
    More visitors carry on into the site instead of leaving straight away.

  2. 2Answers before the questions come up

    What we did
    A row directly below the hero with free delivery, open returns, in-store pickup and free advice.
    Why
    Delivery costs and returns are common reasons to hesitate when shopping online. Here visitors get the answer before the question even arises.
    What it delivers
    Fewer abandoned purchases and more trust in a store people have not heard of before.

  3. 3Shop by room, the way customers think

    What we did
    The categories are rooms, not product types, and every category has a large photo. The living room gets the most space because that is where most people start.
    Why
    People furnishing a home look for something for a room, not for “side tables” or “light sources”. Photos also show the style instantly, without anyone having to read.
    What it delivers
    A shorter path to the right product, and a sense that the store understands how people live.

  4. 4Price and buy button visible right away

    What we did
    The best sellers are shown with photo, material, price and a button that adds the product to the cart. The cart counts up and a short confirmation appears.
    Why
    A hidden price creates uncertainty, and every extra click loses buyers. The confirmation shows the purchase went through, so nobody clicks twice by mistake.
    What it delivers
    More sales, because the path from interest to cart is a single click.

  5. 5Inspiration you can buy

    What we did
    A large photo of a furnished room with clickable points. Each point shows the product, the price and a buy button.
    Why
    Interior customers often buy a whole look they have seen, not a single product. Here the inspiration leads straight to a purchase instead of a search somewhere else.
    What it delivers
    More products per purchase, because the whole room is one click away.

  6. 6What the online giants cannot offer

    What we did
    Free design advice has its own section with a booking form on the page. Customers choose where to meet and enter their name and email, nothing more.
    Why
    Personal service is a local store's greatest strength. Every field in a form means fewer people complete it, so we only ask for what is needed to get in touch.
    What it delivers
    More booked meetings, and a meeting with a designer often leads to a larger purchase.

  7. 7Other customers' words carry the most weight

    What we did
    One large review and two short ones, with names and what each customer bought, plus the average rating below.
    Why
    What the store says about itself is advertising. What customers say is proof. Short quotes get read, long ones do not.
    What it delivers
    More trust, right before the visitor makes up their mind.

  8. 8From the screen into the store

    What we did
    Opening hours, address and a directions button, alongside a photo from inside the store. The opening hours are also included in the code in a way Google can read.
    Why
    Many people check opening hours on their phone just before they leave. The photo shows what it looks like inside, which makes it easier to walk in.
    What it delivers
    More store visits, and the right opening hours directly in Google's results.

Under the surface. What visitors do not see, but notice.

  • Built for mobile first

    Most visits happen on a phone. The buttons are large enough for a thumb, products can be swiped through and the menu is one tap away.

  • Fast to load

    Images load only when they are needed and the fonts are hosted on the site. A slow page loses visitors before it has even appeared.

  • Easy for Google to understand

    Headings in the right order, descriptive image texts and store information that Google can read and show in its results.

  • Accessible to everyone

    Clear contrast, a visible focus outline for keyboard users and calmer motion for anyone who has switched animations off.

  • Dark mode

    The page follows the visitor's setting and switches to dark colours in the evening, without losing its character.

Colours and typefaces. Chosen for a reason.

Many interior design sites look the same, with beige and gold. Norrsund Hem got a deep forest green that connects to Nordic nature and makes the store recognisable.

  • A single accent colour. Amber is only used where we want attention: the cart, the points in the room and the newsletter.
  • Two typefaces. Bricolage Grotesque in the headings adds personality, and Manrope in the body text is easy to read at every size.
  • The same shapes everywhere. Buttons are round, and images and panels have soft corners. That makes the page feel whole.
